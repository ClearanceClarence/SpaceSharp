# Code of conduct

SpaceSharp is a small open-source project, and the places where people meet around it (issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews) should be worth showing up to. This page says what that means and what happens when it doesn't hold.

## What we expect

- Be kind and be direct. Disagree with ideas, not with people. "This approach will break on exFAT because…" is welcome; "this is stupid" is not.
- Assume good intent, and ask before you conclude. Most friction in a project like this is a misunderstanding about what someone meant or tried.
- Help newcomers. Explain the thing, link the file, say which command to run. Nobody is expected to know the codebase, Velopack or the NTFS record format before they can ask a question.
- Keep it about the project. Politics, religion, and other people's personal lives are not topics here.
- Respect maintainers' time and contributors' effort. A pull request that is declined still deserves a reason; a review that asks for changes still deserves a thank you.

## What we don't accept

- Insults, slurs, harassment or threats, in any channel connected to the project.
- Sexualized language or imagery, or unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as a real name, address or employer, without their permission.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, reopening settled decisions to wear people down, or following people to other spaces to continue an argument.
- Anything that would reasonably be considered unacceptable in a professional setting.

## Scope

This applies to the GitHub repository (issues, pull requests, discussions, wiki, releases) and to anywhere someone represents the project, such as a talk or a post that speaks for SpaceSharp. It also applies when project spaces are used to target someone who is not a participant.

## Reporting

If you see or experience something that does not belong here, report it to the maintainer privately through GitHub: open the maintainer's profile at [github.com/ClearanceClarence](https://github.com/ClearanceClarence) and use the contact listed there, or, for anything that is also a security matter, follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

Say what happened, where, and when, and include links or screenshots if you have them. Reports are read by the maintainer only, handled as quickly as possible, and kept confidential as far as the situation allows. You will get a reply.

## What happens next

The maintainer decides the response, in proportion to what happened:

1. **A private word.** For a first slip or a borderline case: an explanation of what crossed the line and why.
2. **A public correction.** For a clear violation in a thread: a note in the thread, and removal or editing of the offending content.
3. **A temporary block.** For repeated behavior after a warning: no interaction with the repository for a set period.
4. **A permanent block.** For harassment, threats, doxxing, or a pattern that does not stop.

Someone who disagrees with a decision can say so in the same private channel; the maintainer will read it, though there is no formal appeals process in a one-person project.
